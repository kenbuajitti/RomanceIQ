# The Last First Night — developer notes (spoilers)

The strongest question is whether Maya can distinguish being needed from
being understood, while being honest about what she wants herself.

Maya, 25, lost her Atlanta design job and is hiding it behind a “sabbatical.”
Her Monday Chicago residency interview is real, but no offer is guaranteed.
She wants a successful cinema campaign and permission to imagine a new future.
The player controls her disclosures, boundaries, loyalties and romantic choices.

Noah, 27, is familiar, funny and generous, but wants the person Maya used to be
to stay and help save the cinema. He took a refundable $4,000 deposit on its
original projector to cover staff wages. That explains his choice without
excusing a fundraising campaign that still promises its preservation.

Eli, 28, recognises Maya's creative ambition, but his film was commissioned as
a farewell to a failing cinema by a redevelopment-funded production company.
He objected to staging failure before meeting Maya, but kept the contract and
withheld its terms. The objection complicates the betrayal rather than erasing it.

A second reversal: Noah secretly wrote the encouragement card Maya credits to
a teacher for helping her leave for design school. His earlier support contrasts
with his present fear of her leaving. Neither fact determines the right partner.

The 20 scene directions live in StoryServer/romance.mjs. Clues are planted early,
financial and filming secrets surface by the midpoint, Maya's honesty comes
under pressure later, and the reopening forces concrete follow-through.
If the player discovers something early, the model is told to advance its
consequences instead of repeating the reveal.

Romance and saving the building are separate outcomes. Both men, neither, or
an unresolved commitment may be emotionally plausible, but the final outcome
must honour earlier boundaries and the final chosen action. The ending includes
a six-week epilogue about all three agendas. No surprise fortune or instant fame.

This prototype deliberately fixes the cast and secret facts. Replay variety
comes from choices and generated scenes, not randomly reassigned motives.
The server retains full accepted history and the fixed bible. This supports
continuity but does not mechanically guarantee that model prose stays consistent;
live playtesting is required before claiming narrative quality or retention.
